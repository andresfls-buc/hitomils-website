Snow changes the way a wedding portrait feels. In Hokkaido, a quiet forest or open winter landscape can make a simple veil, soft lip colour and considered hairstyle feel especially striking. It also asks for planning: you may move between cold outdoor locations, a warm car or studio, and several layers of clothing.

If you are planning a winter 2026–2027 wedding or pre-wedding shoot, share the real schedule and setting with your hair and makeup artist. Your look should suit your features and outfit, and feel comfortable after dressing, travelling and stepping outside.

## Begin with the photographs and the schedule

Winter sessions are already being scheduled for the coming season. Studio SOLA's [Biei winter-shoot announcement](https://photo.decollte.co.jp/stores/18/blogs/21868) describes a 2027 snow photography season from January 12 to March 9. It is one provider's schedule; ask your own photographer about dates, locations and plans, then share those details with your artist.

Send more than a moodboard. Include your getting-ready address, ready-by and departure times, outdoor stops and outfit changes. Tell me how long you expect to be outside and whether there is a warm indoor place between locations. Confirm transport, access, clothing and weather alternatives with your photographer or organiser.

Sapporo's official tourism guide advises visitors to prepare for cold outdoors, warm indoor spaces and winter footwear with grip. Its [winter clothing guide](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/spot/feature/clothes_winter/) can help with layers and shoes. Ask your photographer how practical footwear fits the route and timing.

## Choose makeup for snowy light and your own colouring

Snow reflects a lot of light, so the camera may read colour and contrast differently from a softly lit room. At your trial, check your makeup in daylight and take photographs from a few distances. The goal is to keep the balance you like visible in the setting and final images.

Start with skin comfort and the finish you enjoy. If your skin feels dry in winter, tell your artist what products you use and what feels comfortable. We can discuss preparation for your routine and planned makeup. A satin or softly luminous finish can read beautifully in snow; powder can be placed where needed. Your complexion, preferences and the photographer's lighting matter more than a fixed winter formula.

Think about colour in relation to your face, outfit and flowers. More eye definition, a soft rose cheek or a lip colour visible in a bright landscape may work well; test these choices rather than treating them as rules. Bring outfit or bouquet colours, and agree with your artist on a touch-up lip shade and finish.

Makeup can shift as you dress, travel and move between temperatures. Ask what the service includes and whether accompaniment or touch-ups during photography can be arranged and quoted for your date and location. If the artist will not be present, agree on a touch-up plan and who will carry the items. Avoid trying unfamiliar skincare or makeup just before the shoot.

## Plan hair around movement, veil and outerwear

> **Q: When preparing a bride for a wedding or photo shoot in the snow in Hokkaido, what do you do differently with the makeup or hairstyle to adapt to the cold?**
>
> **Hitomi:** _"Well for the case of grooming the hair during winter times we need to do this indoors , to make sure everything can be done properly when this is ready then we move outside to start the photoshotings."_

Plan an indoor getting-ready space where the hairstyle can be completed before you head outside. Allow time for dressing and travel between the appointment and the first outdoor photographs.

Your hairstyle needs to work with the photographs and with the way you put on and remove warm layers. A cape, coat or scarf can catch loose hair; a veil may change how a style sits or how quickly you can change looks. Bring the veil and accessories to your trial, and show your artist the neckline and outer layer.

Loose waves give movement, but wind and clothing may disturb hair around the face. A half-up shape keeps some hair away; a chignon or secured updo can be easier to manage with layers and a veil. Ask your artist to check the style from every angle and practise putting on the cape or coat without pulling at pins.

[ANNIVERSAIRE's June 2026 article](https://www.anniversaire.co.jp/brand/omotte/magazine/preparation/21346/) discusses ribbon and sleek chignons, S-waves and bob styling. Treat these references as starting points; I would adapt a style to your hair, accessories, outerwear and time for changes.

## Make the outdoor-to-indoor transitions part of the plan

Include dressing, travel, setup and outfit or hair changes in the schedule. Ask your photographer where you can warm up and how much time is available to check the look between locations. Planned pauses make it easier to settle hair and makeup before close-up portraits.

Tell your artist about glasses, a veil, hat or cape, and whether you will remove and replace them. Bring a reference image of the finished look, plus photos of your clothing and accessories. At a trial, we can discuss what might move, pin placement and which details matter most if the schedule changes.
